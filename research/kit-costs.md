# Wayside Street-Aid Pilot — Kit Cost Research (Delhi, 2026)

**Prepared:** 2026-08-04 · All prices in INR (₹), accessed 2026-08-04 via web search of Indian retail/wholesale listings. Delhi wholesale markets (Bhagirath Palace for medical, Sadar Bazaar for general goods) publish no online price lists; IndiaMART/bulk-pack rates are used as the wholesale proxy. Every price carries its source URL. Items where no direct listing was found are marked **(est.)** with the basis stated.

**Wholesale assumption (stated up front):** where a direct bulk/trade quote was found (IndiaMART, box-rate), that number is used. Where only retail MRP was found, wholesale = retail × 0.70 — a **30% bulk discount**, conservative for NGO-scale cash purchases (20–50 units) at Bhagirath Palace / Sadar Bazaar, where deeper discounts are common on medical consumables.

## 6. Honest-uncertainty note

These are desk-research prices assembled from US-routed searches of Indian e-commerce and B2B listings on 2026-08-04, not quotations: online MRPs and flash-sale discounts (1mg, BigBasket, Blinkit, Flipkart) move weekly, IndiaMART "starting from" trade rates assume negotiation and often exclude GST and delivery, and the pilot's true costs will only be known after a physical price-check at Bhagirath Palace (medical) and Sadar Bazaar (tarpaulin, rope, plastic) — for which no online price lists exist. Several figures are explicitly estimated: the 6×4 tarpaulin retail price (no exact-size listing found; anchored to per-sq-ft wholesale and adjacent-size retail), chlorine-tablet per-strip cost (listings omit pack counts), plastic sheeting (derived from per-kg LDPE rates), water-pouch street retail, and all "30% off MRP" wholesale entries. Two substitutions could move totals materially: generic ORS instead of branded Electral (trade prices of ₹5–10/sachet are common, which would cut MED ~₹20 and FOOD ~₹12) and a true heat-and-eat 300 g meal instead of the 60 g add-hot-water poha (which would raise FOOD by ₹60–100/kit — the 60 g pouch also assumes access to hot water, a real constraint for street distribution). Finally, tarpaulin and repellent prices spike 10–30% at monsoon onset (June–July) in Delhi, exactly when SEAS kits are needed, so pre-monsoon procurement (March–April) is advised; the 30%/month replenishment rate is a planning assumption, not observed demand, and should be recalibrated after the first month of pilot data.
